Dubdi Monastery

Location: Yuksom, West Sikkim, India | Nearest Town: Yuksom (3 km)

❮ ❯

📜 History

Dubdi Monastery, also known as Yuksom Monastery, was established in 1701 by Chogyal Namgyal, the first king of Sikkim. It is the oldest monastery in Sikkim and is part of the Buddhist pilgrimage circuit including Norbugang Chorten, Pemayangtse Monastery, Rabdentse ruins, Sanga Choeling Monastery, Khecheopalri Lake, and Tashiding Monastery. It belongs to the Nyingma sect of Tibetan Buddhism.

🏛️ Architecture

  • Perched at 2,100 meters atop a hill near Yuksom, surrounded by lush forests.
  • Two-story traditional Tibetan building with a square plan and south-facing entrance.
  • Flat iron-sheet roof with tapering tower and bell-shaped gilded dome ("Gyaltshen").
  • Interior houses images of divinities, saints, ritual texts, and statues of founding lamas.
  • Accessible via a 3 km trek from Yuksom.

🎉 Festivals

  • Bhumchhu Festival: 14th–15th day of 1st Tibetan month (February).
  • Sacred water vessel ritual predicts agricultural prospects for the year.
  • Religious rituals and prayers attract devotees from across the region.

🕒 Visitor Information

  • Opening Hours: Daily 6:00 AM – 6:00 PM
  • Entry Fee: Free
  • Activities: Trek through scenic trails, explore monastery interior, observe rituals, enjoy views of Mount Kanchenjunga, interact with resident monks.
